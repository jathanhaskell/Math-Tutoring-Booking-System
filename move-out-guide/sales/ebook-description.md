# LISTING 1 — The Move-Out Blueprint *(core edition)*

**Positioned to the parent. Written for their kid.** That split is deliberate: a
book that sounds like it was written for their parent doesn't get read past page 3.

### Headline

> ## Your kid wants to move out. They think it costs $3,000. It's closer to $7,200.
> **The 14-step system that gets them out — and keeps them out.**

### Sub-headline

Between 40 and 60% of young adults who move out end up moving back in at least
once. This is the guide built to prevent that.

### Body

Here's what nobody tells you about your adult child still living at home:

**It's probably not motivation.** About 70% of 25-to-34-year-olds living with their
parents have jobs. There are 25.2 million U.S. adults under 35 in the same
situation — roughly one in three aged 18–34. When a third of a generation does the
same thing, it stops being a character flaw.

**It's the math, and they've got it wrong.** Almost everyone plans around "three
times the rent." The real figure includes utility deposits that cost *more* on a
thin credit file, setup costs nobody lists, and — the one that decides everything —
a full reserve month for when the car breaks in month four.

That gap is why people move out in March and move home in November.

**The Move-Out Blueprint is 50 pages and 14 steps.** No filler, no pep talk. Every
step ends with one specific if-then commitment, because that format produced a
medium-to-large improvement in actually reaching the goal across 94 studies.

#### What's inside

- **Step 1 — Their real Number.** The four-bucket calculation. It usually comes out near double what they guessed
- **Step 2 — A dated deadline**, not "someday"
- **Step 3 — Closing the gap**, with the three levers ranked by actual leverage
- **Step 4 — Credit from zero in six months.** Including the CFPB-researched move most people have never heard of
- **Steps 5–6 — Getting approved**, plus the cover-letter template that beats a better credit score
- **Steps 7–9 — Finding it without getting scammed.** Consumers reported ~$65M in rental-scam losses since 2020; about half started with a fake Facebook ad
- **Steps 10–11 — The move, and the 30 minutes on move-in day that decide whether they get their deposit back**
- **Steps 12–14 — Staying out.** The money system, food, and the part of month three nobody warns them about

#### Why this one is different

**Every number is cited.** 218 sources. Federal agencies — HUD, CFPB, FTC, BLS,
USDA, the Surgeon General's office — plus the Harvard Joint Center for Housing
Studies and peer-reviewed research. The complete list comes with the book, free.

**It contradicts the standard advice where the standard advice is wrong.** Most
guides say download a budgeting app. Research from the Think Forward Initiative and
the University of Arizona found budget-feedback apps can make people spend *more*,
because seeing "amount left to spend" reassures you there's money left to spend. The
system in Step 12 is built around that finding instead of ignoring it.

**It tells you where the evidence is weak.** The included source list has a whole
section on it — including one claim from an earlier draft that had to be softened
after the research came back thinner than expected.

#### Who it's for

Parents buying it for an adult child who's ready to go but stuck on the how. Young
adults buying it for themselves. Anyone who's tried "just save more" and found it
isn't a plan.

#### Who it's not for

Anyone wanting motivation. This is a sequence, not encouragement. And if the honest

# LISTING 2 — The Move-Out Blueprint: Parent Edition

### Headline

> ## You're paying $1,384 a month. You're putting $609 into your own retirement.
> **How to help your adult child move out — without funding it forever.**

### Sub-headline

Help that builds capability, help that creates dependency, and the research on how
to tell which one you're giving.

### Body

Parents supporting an adult child hand over an average of $1,384 a month — more than
twice the $609 the average working parent puts into their own retirement. 58% say
they've sacrificed their own financial security. Nearly nine in ten say they'd
sacrifice more.

Almost none of them have added up the number.

**This is not a book about kicking your kid out.** Pressure doesn't work, and the
research on controlling approaches is genuinely bad — a meta-analysis of 53 studies
found helicopter parenting associated with *reduced self-efficacy*, which is the
exact capacity required to move out. You can make this worse by trying harder.

**It's a book about help that actually works.**

#### What's inside

- **What's really blocking them** — and why "lazy" is the wrong diagnosis for a generation that's ~70% employed
- **What it's costing you**, with the worksheet most parents have never filled in — including the retirement math
- **A readiness score for them — and one for you.** The second one is harder
- **How to open the conversation** without it landing as an eviction notice, including the five sentences that end it before it starts
- **The written agreement**, and "shadow rent" — the structure that builds the habit *and* funds the deposit
- **The help ladder** — what builds capability versus what substitutes for it, drawn from self-determination theory and financial socialisation research
- **Gift, loan, or match** — and why matched saving beats a straight gift
- **Cosigning: the full risk**, stated plainly, with the one question that decides it
- **If they move back** — 40–60% do, and how you respond decides the second attempt
- **Protecting your own retirement**, including the ceiling to set before the next request

#### The uncomfortable premise

If you deplete your retirement helping them, you become their financial
responsibility in twenty years. Protecting yourself isn't the opposite of generosity.
It's the same goal on a longer timeline.

#### Honest about the research

The parenting findings here are correlational. They describe consistent,
cross-culturally replicated associations — not a formula that guarantees a result in
your family. The book says so, in those words. Your child is not an effect size.

**Is this just information I could find free online?**
Every fact in it is publicly available — and that's stated plainly in the book. What
you're buying is the sequence, the filtering, and 218 sources already checked and
tiered. If you'd rather spend forty hours assembling it yourself, you genuinely can.

**Which edition do I need?**
Buying it for your kid → the core edition. Want help with the conversation, what to
fund, and cosigning → the Parent Edition. Doing this together → the bundle.

**My kid won't read it.**
Chapter 4 of the Parent Edition is specifically about that, including how to hand it
over so it doesn't land as a hint. The short version: don't give them the book, do
Step 1 *with* them.

**Will this work if they can't afford to move out at all?**
Step 1 will tell you honestly, including when the answer is "not this year." That's a
real result, not a failure — leaving with a cushion beats leaving in March and
returning in November.

**Is it US-specific?**
Yes. Dollar figures, tenant law, credit bureaus and assistance programmes are all
US-based. The behavioural methods travel; the numbers don't.

**How long is it?**
Core: 50 pages. Parent Edition: 41. Both deliberately short — you're buying a
solved problem, not a reading assignment. The Research Basis adds 41 more if you
want to check the work.
